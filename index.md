## Why people look for a Geek Squad alternative

Geek Squad, Best Buy's support service, works well for plenty of people. These are the most common reasons people look elsewhere:

- **The membership.** Best Buy's 24/7 remote support and unlimited computer service come with its My Best Buy Total membership. If you only need one problem fixed, a yearly membership can feel like more than you need.
- **Waiting at the store.** Bringing a computer in can mean days without it.
- **No computer house calls under the membership.** According to Best Buy's membership page, house calls under My Best Buy Total cover appliances and larger TVs, not computers.
- **Fake "Geek Squad" scams.** Criminals send fake Geek Squad renewal emails and list fake phone numbers. Some people would simply rather deal with a smaller company they can reach directly.

## 2. HelloTech: best for in-home help across the US

HelloTech has technicians across the country who come to your home for computers, printers, Wi-Fi, TVs and smart home devices. Its online computer support can be booked the same day and runs 24/7.

## 3. Geeks on Site: best for help at any hour

Geeks on Site offers remote repair for PCs and Macs around the clock, plus onsite visits for homes and businesses nationwide, often the same day. It also covers printers, networks and smart home devices, and offers a 100% satisfaction guarantee.

- **Choose it if:** your computer tends to break at 11 p.m.

## 4. Rescuecom: best for unlimited remote fixes

Rescuecom has been fixing computers since 1997. Its home plan offers unlimited remote repair, 24 hours a day, and it claims a large onsite network.

This Los Angeles company works entirely online. A technician connects to your Windows PC or Mac and fixes it while you watch, for a flat fee rather than an hourly rate, with a promise that you don't pay if it isn't fixed.

## How to avoid fake Geek Squad and tech support scams

The FTC's guide to [spotting and avoiding tech support scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-spot-avoid-and-report-tech-support-scams) lists the warning signs. In short:

1. Real companies don't call, email or text you out of the blue about a computer problem.
2. Real security warnings never tell you to call a phone number.
3. Nobody legitimate asks for payment in gift cards, wire transfers or cryptocurrency.
4. Find support numbers yourself, on the company's official website. Never use one from a pop-up, an email or a search ad you're unsure about.

Microsoft has more advice on [protecting yourself from tech support scams](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/protect-yourself-from-tech-support-scams-2ebf91bd-f94c-2a8a-e541-f5c800d18435). You can report scams at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).
